Check a validity period, a certificate's for instance, against the time.

While the clock has a lower bound only, a period is valid unless it ended before it: a period starting later cannot be told from one that has started. ebisu_time_from_civil() converts the dates a TLS stack gives.

◆ ebisu_clock_forget_time()

void ebisu_clock_forget_time ( ebisu_clock_t * clock)

Forget the time the clock gave, with its doubt, if it has one.

For the socket callbacks to call when their TLS stack refuses the server's certificate as not valid yet, at a time the clock gave as exact. On a device whose monotonic clock stops while it sleeps, ebisu_server_clock_t falls behind, and a server that renewed its certificate meanwhile presents one that starts after it. Without a time of the server's, the clock could not catch up: the connection that would bring one is refused. Forgotten, the time becomes a lower bound, which ebisu_clock_check_validity() and the socket callbacks do not refuse a certificate for not having started by, so the next connection goes through, and its response gives the clock the time again.

The sockets call it only when the dates are all the certificate is refused for. One that the CA certificates don't lead to, or for another host, says nothing of the time: anyone can make a certificate of any dates.

A clock without doubt, the system's, is left as it is.

Parameters
[in]clockThe clock.

◆ ebisu_clock_from_system_time()

bool ebisu_clock_from_system_time ( int64_t seconds,
int64_t * out_now )

What a clock that reads a time of day kept elsewhere gives: the system's, or a real-time clock's.

A time earlier than shortly before the library's source was built is not the time: nothing built from the source runs earlier. It is a time not set, as a device without a real-time clock has at boot, 1970 or 2000, until NTP sets it, or a real-time clock that lost its battery. Against it, every certificate would be refused as not valid yet. So that one gives the floor ebisu_server_clock_init() starts a clock with instead, as a lower bound.

ebisu_system_clock() of the POSIX and Win32 ports gives its time this way, and an application's clock over a real-time clock can too.

Parameters
[in]secondsThe time of day read, seconds since 1970-01-01T00:00:00Z.
[out]out_nowWhat the clock gives: seconds, or the floor.
Returns
true if out_now is seconds, the time; false if it is the floor, a lower bound.
