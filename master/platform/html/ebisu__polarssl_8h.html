Detailed Description

TLS with PolarSSL 1.3, over another socket of the system's: experimental.

As the mbedTLS port's socket, ebisu_mbedtls.h, for PolarSSL 1.3, mbedTLS's name until 1.3.10, whose API mbedTLS 1.3 kept until its last release, 1.3.22: for vendors' SDKs that ship it, some with its cryptography in their chips' ROM. Experimental: its API may change. PolarSSL 1.3 is past its end of life: its last release, 1.3.22, came in February 2018, and 1.3.8, of July 2014, lacks the security fixes of each of the 14 since. Use the mbedTLS port where mbedTLS 2.4 or later can be had.

Settings of the application's for each connection.

Called with each connection's context, after the port's own settings and before the handshake, for those of the application's: cipher suites, with ssl_set_ciphersuites(), or, for PolarSSL built with SSL_MAX_CONTENT_LEN smaller than the 16 KB a server may send, the maximum fragment length to ask the server for, with ssl_set_max_frag_len(), which a server that doesn't take the extension ignores, where a record longer than PolarSSL takes fails the connection, as the socket's logger says, with the record's length; and the random generator, with ssl_set_rng(), where EBISU_POLARSSL_SETS_RNG is 0. typedef struct ebisu_polarssl_socket_t ebisu_polarssl_socket_t

A TLS connection with PolarSSL, over another socket of the system's.

The socket under it connects to the host, sends and receives the TLS records, and closes: a socket of plain TCP, such as ebisu_posix_tcp_socket_t, whose timeouts are the connection's. A receive that times out is EBISU_SOCK_FAIL, which recv_timed_out tells from a lost connection, and leaves the connection as it was. A send that times out fails too.

The server's certificate is checked as the configuration says, and the validity period of each of its chain's at the time of the clock set by ebisu_polarssl_socket_set_clock(), in place of the check PolarSSL makes itself with POLARSSL_HAVE_TIME. Without a clock, PolarSSL checks them at the system's time, with POLARSSL_HAVE_TIME; without that either, the socket doesn't connect, rather than not check them.

A close_notify of the server's, or the end of the stream under it, is the end of the stream. recv_wait, when the socket under it has one, counts what PolarSSL has received and not yet returned.

Why an operation failed, its last_error says: what the socket under it said, for what failed there, as a host that didn't resolve or an address that refused, a certificate refused, with PolarSSL's verify flags, BADCERT_*, a server with no TLS version or cipher suite in common with the configuration, EBISU_SOCK_ERROR_TLS_MISMATCH, with PolarSSL's error, another TLS failure, with PolarSSL's error, a timeout, or a peer that closed. The TLS failures also go to the logger set by ebisu_polarssl_socket_set_logger(), if any, with a line for the mismatch that says so; the socket under it logs its own, to the logger it is given.

Each connection takes PolarSSL's buffers, of SSL_BUFFER_LEN each way, 16 KB and some by default, from the heap, with polarssl_malloc(). One serves one connection at a time, from one task at a time, and is used again for the next once closed. Initialize with ebisu_polarssl_socket_init().

◆ ebisu_polarssl_tls_t

typedef struct ebisu_polarssl_tls_t ebisu_polarssl_tls_t

The TLS configuration connections share.

TLS 1.2, with PolarSSL's default cipher suites, which a server picks one of. The server's certificate has to chain to one of the configuration's CA certificates, and be issued for the host connected to, unless ebisu_polarssl_tls_set_verify() says otherwise. PolarSSL 1.3 has no configuration shared by connections, as mbedTLS 2 on has: the port keeps this one, and sets each connection's context from it as it connects.

Random numbers come from a CTR_DRBG of the configuration's own, which ebisu_polarssl_tls_init() seeds from PolarSSL's entropy sources, the hardware's with POLARSSL_ENTROPY_HARDWARE_ALT. PolarSSL built without POLARSSL_CTR_DRBG_C or POLARSSL_ENTROPY_C, as some of vendors' SDKs configure it, has neither, and EBISU_POLARSSL_SETS_RNG is 0: the application sets the generator, with ssl_set_rng() in its setup, which ebisu_polarssl_tls_set_setup() sets, and a connection fails without one. TLS's keys are made from these numbers, whichever gives them: the entropy sources have to give true randomness, and the application's generator has to be cryptographically secure, a hardware random number generator's, or a DRBG seeded from one. Connections share the generator: on more than one task at once, PolarSSL has to be built with POLARSSL_THREADING_C, with POLARSSL_THREADING_PTHREAD on POSIX, or POLARSSL_THREADING_ALT and functions of the system's, which the port locks its CTR_DRBG with, as PolarSSL's own doesn't; or the application's generator has to take calls from more than one task at once.

Initialize with ebisu_polarssl_tls_init(), before the connections, and free with ebisu_polarssl_tls_free() once none is open.

ebisu_clock_t * clock )

Sets the clock the server's certificate is checked at the time of.

While the clock has only a lower bound of the time, a certificate that expired before it is refused, and one not valid yet is not, as it may have been issued since. A certificate not valid yet at a time the clock had is refused, and the clock's time forgotten, so that the next connection is checked at a lower bound and its response gives the clock the time again: more likely than a certificate from the future, the time fell behind. See ebisu_clock_forget_time().

Parameters
[out]sockThe socket.
[in]clockThe clock, which has to outlive the socket, or NULL, as the socket starts, for the system's time.

bool ebisu_polarssl_tls_set_verify ( ebisu_polarssl_tls_t * tls,
ebisu_tls_verify_t verify,
const unsigned char * pin )

Sets how the configuration's connections check the server's certificate.

Every connection is checked in full unless this says otherwise: see ebisu_tls_verify_t for what each mode checks, and what it doesn't. With EBISU_TLS_VERIFY_CHAIN, the chain and the dates are checked as in full, at the clock's time; with EBISU_TLS_VERIFY_PIN or EBISU_TLS_VERIFY_NONE, they aren't, the configuration's CA certificates aren't used, and a socket without a clock connects without the system's time too. A pin is the SHA-256 of the server's certificate in DER, or of its public key's SubjectPublicKeyInfo in DER, which PolarSSL doesn't keep, and is written again with POLARSSL_PK_WRITE_C, or else not taken. Set it before the first connection.

Parameters
[in,out]tlsThe configuration.
[in]verifyHow servers' certificates are checked.
[in]pinWith EBISU_TLS_VERIFY_PIN, the EBISU_TLS_PIN_SIZE bytes of the pin; otherwise ignored, and may be NULL.
Returns
true, or false, leaving the configuration as it was, for EBISU_TLS_VERIFY_PIN without a pin, or a mode that isn't one.
