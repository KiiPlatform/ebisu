typedef struct ebisu_mbedtls_socket_t ebisu_mbedtls_socket_t

A TLS connection with mbedTLS, over another socket of the system's.

The socket under it connects to the host, sends and receives the TLS records, and closes: a socket of plain TCP, such as ebisu_posix_tcp_socket_t, whose timeouts are the connection's. A receive that times out is EBISU_SOCK_FAIL, which recv_timed_out tells from a lost connection, and leaves the connection as it was. A send that times out fails too.

The server's certificate is checked as the configuration says, and the validity period of each of its chain's at the time of the clock set by ebisu_mbedtls_socket_set_clock(), in place of the check mbedTLS makes itself with MBEDTLS_HAVE_TIME_DATE. Without a clock, mbedTLS checks them at the system's time, with MBEDTLS_HAVE_TIME_DATE; without that either, the socket doesn't connect, rather than not check them.

A close_notify of the server's, or the end of the stream under it, is the end of the stream. A TLS 1.3 server's session tickets are taken and dropped. recv_wait, when the socket under it has one, counts what mbedTLS has received and not yet returned.

With mbedTLS before 2.18, which sets a verify callback for a configuration only, the socket has a copy of the configuration's of its own, taken at each connect, with its own callback.

Why an operation failed, its last_error says: what the socket under it said, for what failed there, as a host that didn't resolve or an address that refused, a certificate refused, with mbedTLS's verify flags, MBEDTLS_X509_BADCERT_*, a server with no TLS version or cipher suite in common with the configuration, EBISU_SOCK_ERROR_TLS_MISMATCH, with mbedTLS's error, another TLS failure, with mbedTLS's error, a timeout, or a peer that closed. The TLS failures also go to the logger set by ebisu_mbedtls_socket_set_logger(), if any, with a line for the mismatch that says so; the socket under it logs its own, to the logger it is given.

One serves one connection at a time, from one task at a time, and is used again for the next once closed. Initialize with ebisu_mbedtls_socket_init().

◆ ebisu_mbedtls_tls_t

typedef struct ebisu_mbedtls_tls_t ebisu_mbedtls_tls_t

The TLS configuration connections share.

TLS 1.2 or later, 1.3 where mbedTLS is built with it, with mbedTLS's default cipher suites. The server's certificate has to chain to one of the configuration's CA certificates, and be issued for the host connected to, unless ebisu_mbedtls_tls_set_verify() says otherwise.

Random numbers come from PSA's generator, which ebisu_mbedtls_tls_init() starts with psa_crypto_init(), from mbedTLS's entropy sources: on a device, the hardware's, as mbedTLS is configured to take them, with MBEDTLS_ENTROPY_HARDWARE_ALT in mbedTLS 3.6, or MBEDTLS_PSA_DRIVER_GET_ENTROPY in 4.x. With mbedTLS 2, they come from a CTR_DRBG of the configuration's own, which ebisu_mbedtls_tls_init() seeds from mbedTLS's entropy sources, the hardware's with MBEDTLS_ENTROPY_HARDWARE_ALT. mbedTLS 2 built without MBEDTLS_CTR_DRBG_C or MBEDTLS_ENTROPY_C, as some of vendors' SDKs configure it, has neither, and EBISU_MBEDTLS_SETS_RNG is 0: the application sets the generator, with mbedtls_ssl_conf_rng() on ebisu_mbedtls_tls_config(), before the first connection, which fails without one. TLS's keys are made from these numbers, whichever gives them: the entropy sources have to give true randomness, and the application's generator has to be cryptographically secure, a hardware random number generator's, or a DRBG seeded from one. Connections share the generator: on more than one task at once, mbedTLS has to be built with MBEDTLS_THREADING_C, with MBEDTLS_THREADING_PTHREAD on POSIX, or MBEDTLS_THREADING_ALT and functions of the system's, or the application's generator has to take calls from more than one task at once.

mbedTLS 3.6 and 4.x, and mbedTLS 2 from 2.4, as vendors' SDKs ship it, which has TLS 1.2 at the most. mbedTLS 2 is past its end of life: its last release, 2.28.10, came in March 2025, and an earlier 2 lacks the fixes since it, 2.4.0, of 2016, those of most of a decade. Initialize with ebisu_mbedtls_tls_init(), before the connections, and free with ebisu_mbedtls_tls_free() once none is open.

void ebisu_mbedtls_socket_set_clock ( ebisu_mbedtls_socket_t * sock,
ebisu_clock_t * clock )

Sets the clock the server's certificate is checked at the time of.

While the clock has only a lower bound of the time, a certificate that expired before it is refused, and one not valid yet is not, as it may have been issued since. A certificate not valid yet at a time the clock had is refused, and the clock's time forgotten, so that the next connection is checked at a lower bound and its response gives the clock the time again: more likely than a certificate from the future, the time fell behind. See ebisu_clock_forget_time().

Parameters
[out]sockThe socket.
[in]clockThe clock, which has to outlive the socket, or NULL, as the socket starts, for the system's time.

◆ ebisu_mbedtls_socket_set_handshake_timeout()

void ebisu_mbedtls_socket_set_handshake_timeout ( ebisu_mbedtls_socket_t * sock,
ebisu_system_t * system,
unsigned int seconds )

Sets how long the TLS handshake may take.

The socket under it bounds each wait for the server with its receive timeout, and not how many there are in a handshake: a server that takes the connection and says nothing to a socket with no receive timeout, or sends a little at a time, keeps connect from returning. With this set, the handshake fails as a timeout, EBISU_SOCK_ERROR_TIMEOUT, once it has taken this long, by the system's monotonic_ms.

It waits for the server with the recv_wait of the socket under it, for what is left of the time, where that socket has one, as platform's have: its receive timeout doesn't bound a wait of the handshake's then. Without recv_wait, the time is looked at before each receive and send, so a wait lasts as long as the socket under it lets it, and the handshake ends once that wait does.

Unset, as the socket starts, the handshake is bounded by the socket under it alone. Set it before connecting.

Parameters
[out]sockThe socket.
[in]systemThe system, for its monotonic_ms, which has to outlive the socket. With NULL, or one without monotonic_ms, there is no bound.
[in]secondsHow long, in seconds, or 0 for no bound.

mbedtls_ssl_config * ebisu_mbedtls_tls_config ( ebisu_mbedtls_tls_t * tls)

mbedTLS's configuration, for settings of the application's.

Such as the cipher suites, mbedtls_ssl_conf_ciphersuites(), or the lowest version, mbedtls_ssl_conf_min_tls_version(), mbedtls_ssl_conf_min_version() in mbedTLS 2. Or, for an mbedTLS built with a record smaller than the 16 KB a server may send, MBEDTLS_SSL_IN_CONTENT_LEN or, before 2.12, MBEDTLS_SSL_MAX_CONTENT_LEN, the maximum fragment length to ask the server for, mbedtls_ssl_conf_max_frag_len(), which a server that doesn't take the extension ignores. A record longer than mbedTLS takes fails the connection, and the socket's logger says so, with the record's length. Changed after ebisu_mbedtls_tls_init(), before the first connection. What ebisu_mbedtls_tls_init() set, the verification, the CA certificates and the random generator, are the socket's to keep, but for the random generator where EBISU_MBEDTLS_SETS_RNG is 0, which the application sets here, with mbedtls_ssl_conf_rng().

Parameters
[in]tlsThe configuration.
Returns
mbedTLS's configuration.

const unsigned char * ca,
size_t ca_size,
int * out_error )

Sets up a TLS configuration, with the CA certificates the server's has to chain to.

With mbedTLS 3.6 or 4.x, calls psa_crypto_init(), which may have been called already, and doesn't call mbedtls_psa_crypto_free(), which the application may, once it no longer uses PSA. With mbedTLS 2, seeds the configuration's CTR_DRBG, which fails without an entropy source mbedTLS takes for a strong one, but where EBISU_MBEDTLS_SETS_RNG is 0: there it sets no random generator, which the application sets.

Parameters
[out]tlsThe configuration.
[in]caThe CA certificates: PEM, one or more, with the terminating NUL, or one in DER. NULL, with a ca_size of 0, for none, for a configuration that pins the server's certificate or checks none, with ebisu_mbedtls_tls_set_verify(): checked in full, no server's is taken.
[in]ca_sizeThe size of ca, its NUL included for PEM.
[out]out_errorMbedTLS's or PSA's error when it fails, and 0 when it doesn't: which certificate it couldn't read, or that it has no entropy source, for a log. NULL for none.
Returns
Whether it did. If not, the configuration holds nothing to free.

◆ ebisu_mbedtls_tls_set_verify()

bool ebisu_mbedtls_tls_set_verify ( ebisu_mbedtls_tls_t * tls,
ebisu_tls_verify_t verify,
const unsigned char * pin )

Sets how the configuration's connections check the server's certificate.

Every connection is checked in full unless this says otherwise: see ebisu_tls_verify_t for what each mode checks, and what it doesn't. With EBISU_TLS_VERIFY_CHAIN, the chain and the dates are checked as in full, at the clock's time; with EBISU_TLS_VERIFY_PIN or EBISU_TLS_VERIFY_NONE, they aren't, the configuration's CA certificates aren't used, and a socket without a clock connects without the system's time too. A pin is the SHA-256 of the server's certificate in DER, or of its public key's SubjectPublicKeyInfo in DER, which mbedTLS before 2.17 doesn't keep, and is written again with MBEDTLS_PK_WRITE_C, or else not taken. Set it before the first connection.

Parameters
[in,out]tlsThe configuration.
[in]verifyHow servers' certificates are checked.
[in]pinWith EBISU_TLS_VERIFY_PIN, the EBISU_TLS_PIN_SIZE bytes of the pin; otherwise ignored, and may be NULL.
Returns
true, or false, leaving the configuration as it was, for EBISU_TLS_VERIFY_PIN without a pin, or a mode that isn't one.
